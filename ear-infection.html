All about ear infections

Understanding the three distinct anatomical classifications of ear infections.

Outer

Inflammation of the ear canal skin, typically caused by bacteria and fungus. Known as Swimmer's Ear or Otitis Externa.

Middle

Infection located behind the ear drum, common in children and associated with upper respiratory tract infections (Otitis Media).

Inner

Located in the deepest part of the ear and commonly caused by viruses which damage the internal hearing mechanism and nerves.

We treat outer ear infections (Otitis Externa)

Outer ear canal infections are also known as Swimmers Ear or Otitis Externa. The symptoms and risk factors of an ear infection are listed below. Each person may experience symptoms differently.

Symptoms

  • Earache or ear pain
  • Fever (young children)
  • Itchy ears
  • Smelly discharge
  • Deafness or blocked ears
  • Dizziness / Tinnitus

Risk factors

  • Trauma to ear canal
  • Excessive ear wax
  • Psoriasis or Eczema
  • Humid environments
  • Swimming
  • Diabetes

Types & Treatment

How are outer ear infections treated?

Treatment includes antibiotic and steroid drops, pain relief and keeping the ear completely dry. Often, the ear canal first needs to be cleaned of debris using microsuction to allow drops to work effectively.

Perichondritis

Inflammation of the skin and cartilage of the outer ear, often from piercings or bites. Requires aggressive topical and oral antibiotics.

At Auris Ear Care, our specialist ENT Doctors can diagnose and treat these in the comfort of your home, 24/7 across London.

Middle Ear (Otitis Media) & Complications

Middle ear infections are common in children (80% by age 3). They often follow colds or sore throats. While most settle naturally, complications can arise.

Mastoiditis Warning

Rarely, infection spreads to the bone behind the ear (Mastoiditis). This is a medical emergency. Any patient with mastoiditis should be seen in an A&E department immediately.

Ear Infection FAQ

Can you prescribe antibiotics?

Yes, our clinic lead is a GMC-registered ENT Doctor who can issue private prescriptions for antibiotics or antifungal ear drops if they are clinically indicated after your assessment.

Should I use microsuction if I have an infection?

Absolutely. Clearing the infected debris and wax via microsuction is often the most critical step in allowing the prescribed medicine to reach the ear canal skin and effectively treat the infection.

Can I fly with an ear infection?

It is generally advised to avoid flying with an active infection due to potential pain and risk of eardrum perforation from pressure changes. Our doctor can provide a safe travel assessment during your visit.
